Chapter 4: The Geometry of Dawn

In the quiet stillness of the early morning, light acts not merely as a visibility tool, but as a sculptor. It carves out shapes from the darkness, redefining the boundaries of a room before the first mechanical switch is ever flipped. The architecture of a space is fundamentally incomplete without this temporal interaction.

To design for light is to design for time. Every window is a calendar, every shadow a clock. When Eliza Reed argues that the modern facade is a living organ, she refers specifically to the translucent materials that breathe photons into the structural skeleton. This interplay creates a psychological resonance that transcends mere utility.
